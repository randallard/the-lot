## The second half of that sentence is the important one

The fist bump being right is the day's *goal*. The Allemande Left being right is the day's
**evidence**, and it is evidence for the change that had the least to show for itself.

ADR-0017 split every arm rig into a pinned shoulder holding a forearm group, redefined
`ArmPose` from an arm-group origin to an elbow, and converted five call sites and four
components to match. The claim attached to it was that this changes **no pixels** — that the
elbow offset plus the mesh offset composes back to the old mesh offset, that `gripPose` draws
exactly what it drew before, and that the dance floor would look identical. The watch list said
so explicitly: *a Dosado or an Allemande that looks different is a rig-split bug, not a design
change.*

The Allemande Left was render-validated back on 2026-07-27, and it is the one call whose CCW
turn and forearm grip Ryan has checked most carefully. It still looks right. So the rig change
did what it claimed, measured against the strictest thing available.

Worth sitting with, because it is the opposite of how this subsystem's defects have gone. The
pattern all week has been *green tests, wrong render*. This is the case where the arithmetic
said nothing should change and nothing did — and the only way to know that was to look at a
thing the change was not about.

## What is now render-validated

- The approach (ADR-0018) — the pair walk into position rather than being lined up by hand.
- The twist (ADR-0019) — the working shoulder turns toward the partner, spends only what the
  distance asks for, and unwinds as the hand comes away.
- The punch (ADR-0020) — fists meeting head on, straight wrists, at shoulder height.
- The two-segment rig (ADR-0017) — by the Allemande, negatively and conclusively.

## What is shipped and still unexercised, which is the honest other half

🔴 **`aim: "natural"` and everything under it — `reachPose`, `ELBOW_SWING`, the two-bone
solve — has never been rendered.** The built-in fist bump authors `"along-axis"`, and it is the
only contact move that exists. So the solver written this morning, the one the whole ADR-0017
argument was built around, is now shipped behind a flag nothing sets.

By this repo's own standing rule that is not a seam. It is tested, typechecked and plausible,
and those three things have between them failed to catch a render defect four times this week.
ADR-0020 already carries the promotion condition; what this entry adds is that the condition is
**already met in spirit** — the code exists and nothing checks it. The first authored move that
wants a bent arm is what will find out whether it works.

There is a mild irony worth recording: ADR-0017 chose the two-bone solve over the cheaper fix
specifically to make a wrong shoulder *inexpressible*, and the rig split is what delivered that.
The solver itself turned out to be the part that got replaced eight hours later.
