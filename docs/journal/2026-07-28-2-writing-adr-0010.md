# 2026-07-28 (2) — Writing ADR-0010, and what the writing found

*Documents the ADR landed alongside this entry. Continues from
[the buttons were lying](2026-07-28-the-buttons-were-lying.md).*

The contract promised by `DanceFloor.tsx`'s header since M4, written at last, and written
*after* the render was watched rather than before — which was the whole reason it sat
reserved in the ADR index for two days.

The shape was already known going in: three channel kinds, **owned** / **limited** /
**free**, with clipping as the conflict rule. What I did not expect was that writing it
down would change the table twice.

## "Lean is free" was wrong

The 2026-07-27 sketch listed lean among the free channels, and I had repeated that in
PROGRESS.md twice without checking it. But `rigidParts` — the ADR-0012 silhouette the frame
scale is measured from — reads this:

```ts
const lateralLean = Math.sin(deg2rad(Math.abs(body.leanZ))) * (body.height / 2);
```

A sideways lean **is** lateral reach. So `bodyLeanZ` is a silhouette channel and belongs in
`limited`; only `bodyLeanX`, the forward/back lean, is free, and it is free for the same
reason ADR-0012 excludes forward overhangs from lateral clearance in the first place.

Checking `rigidParts` field by field then caught two more that nobody had listed anywhere:
`headOffsetX` (it enters the head part's radius as `head.radius + |offsetX|`) and
`headOffsetY` (it moves `headCenterY`, which decides *which* parts are close enough in
height to need clearance at all).

## Which turned the classification into a derivation

That is the part worth keeping. The channel list had been a judgement call — "probably the
silhouette deltas too" is how it read on 2026-07-27, and a judgement call is exactly what
drifts. Once three separate channels turned out to be limited for the *same* reason, the
rule states itself:

> **A channel is `limited` exactly when it feeds `rigidParts`.**

Anything an emote can change that the square's spacing was computed from can invalidate that
spacing. Not taste; arithmetic. The silhouette deltas fell out as a special case rather than
needing their own argument, and the next person to add a field to `ResolvedPose` has a test
to apply instead of an opinion to form.

The companion rule handles everything the test can't reach: **an unclassified channel is
owned.** A field nobody has thought about is dropped for a driven dancer until someone
classifies it. Failing toward a formation that holds is the safe direction, and it makes the
omission visible instead of silent.

## `bodyDeltaY` is free, and I want the risk on the record

The bob lifts the whole rig, and `rigidParts` assumes a *fixed* body centre — so a bob is
invisible to the clearance model rather than measured by it. Strictly, the derivation rule
doesn't reach it: it changes the height the silhouette sits at, not the silhouette.

Classified free, with the residual risk named in Consequences: a jump that brings a short
dancer's head up to a tall dancer's height mid-pass is unmodelled. That is deliberately the
same move ADR-0012 made with forward overhangs — accept a bounded gap, write down exactly
where it is, rather than pretend the model is total.

## What the ADR does *not* do

It does not consolidate the arbitration. Arms are handled in `arm-pose.ts`, everything else
inline in `DanceFloor`'s frame loop, and owned channels are enforced by simply not reading
them. That works and is render-verified, but it means "unclassified is owned" currently holds
by *accident of omission* rather than by construction — there is no resolver to add a channel
to and forget. Named in Consequences as owed.

And the `limited` silhouette channels are **decided but not enforced**. Classifying them was
the cheap half. They are still applied unclipped today, so the hole the 2026-07-27 entry
found is still open — it just has a rule now, and a place to be fixed.

## On the one-decision-per-file test

The ADR README is strict: a file with a numbered list of decisions is a policy document, and
the test is "if you can't supersede one part of it, it's too big."

This one passes, but not trivially, so the reasoning is worth recording. The **decision** is
the classification scheme and what each kind means on conflict. The table is that scheme
*applied* to the channels that exist today — reclassifying any single channel is a new ADR
that supersedes this one, and would be a real decision with a real argument. What would have
failed the test is bundling "consolidate into one resolver" in as decision two; that is an
implementation direction, and it is recorded as a consequence rather than as a choice.
